# The Snapshot: 2026

> AI was not used to write this blog post

If you are reading this, you have found the digital home for my progress, my projects, and I imagine the occasional technical headache. Right now, I’m a rising senior at UT San Aantonio, balancing a double major in Cybersecurity and Information Systems while working as an AV Student Manager in the Student Union, and am now the Director for Rowdy CyberCon. It’s a lot of moving parts, but that is one reason I am making this blog. I want to create a place where I can collect all of my previous experiences into one place because I have realized that doing the work is only half of the battle. Documenting the process is what actually makes the knowledge stick, plus I have somewhere to reference back to later. This website is not meant to be a polished manuel or guide, it is a look into my progress from a student to a cybersecurity professional, and I am starting with where I am at today.

My imediate pathway is pretty packed. I am currently preparing for the next Rowdy CyberCon event in November, a quick turn around from the last in April. We decided to make this change because the one thing I would really like to add is a career fair. The mission of Rowdy CyberCon is to empower the next generation of cybersecurity professionals. The best way we can do that is by connecting them with employers to help find the best fit for internships and creating an environment where they can network and meet new people. However, due to hiring cycles, we believe the fall would bxe the best time to host a career fair for cybersecurity positions, hence the change. 

Similarly, we had issues with the UTSA STEM career fair. Because STEM is such a broad field, a majority of the booths were not looking to fill cyber positions, and those who where were also looking for other STEM students. This meant that the recruiter at the booth needed to have general knowledge of the company, but not nessisarily specific information about specific positions. We also hope that by focusing the career fair on cybersecurity and IT positions, companies attending will be able to send hiring managers who may have more specific knowledge and be able to answer more questions. Similarly, they will get a direct, inside look at potential candidates, and the more time they can spend with a student, the better chances the student can get an internship.

Secondly, I am also preparing for my Security+ exam. I am still trying to decide if I would like to get Network+ as well, but if I do decide to, I should get Network+ before Security+ due to the hirearchical nature of CompTIA exams. I would also like to finish the Google Cybersecurity certificate on coursera, I have 1/8 parts completed and I started the second part, so I am thinking about finishing that this summer while I figure out the CompTIA exams. However, I would like to work towards CompTIA asap because I will need to start applying for summer internships in the fall, and I think Security+ will be a big help for my resume.

I am also working on this blog. I decided to build it completely from scratch to gain some basic knowledge about web development. I am interested in going into Penetration testing, so I should at least know my way around website file structures. Nowdays, a lot of web development uses frameworks like React which I do not plan to explore with my website, however a lot of the principals are still similar. Secondly, this will give me complete control over my website and allow me customize and document everything I do rather than using a tool to create my blog for me. Also, as a side note, I am looking into creating my own font using my handwriting because I think it would be cool to personalize my website, but I am unsure where I would include that right now.

I am also looking ahead to my career goals and potential masters programs. But one place I know I want to get into is a career in public service. My background as an Eagle Scout has driven my interest in that kind of work, and my goal is to apply my technical skills to defend our national interests. I am writing this first blog post as a sort of time capsule so I can track how my goals evolve over time. I do not have every technical answer yet, but as I progress with my homelab and class assignments, I plan to use this space to log what I learn. This is ultimately where the name "Triage Notes" comes from. I expect this to be very quick and dirty like a medical triage, but it will also by my personal notes to myself.

## Where I am at
As an AV Student manager at the UTSA Student Union, I work with a team of about 15-20 people to set up events on campus. I have really enjoyed my job thus far, especially our big events. We have one big event each semester where we get to go all out and use all of our equipment. We start setting up at 7am until the event starts in the evening which requires a lot of planning and teamwork to pull everything off in time. I have found that I really enjoy working in live production environments, especially when we worked with ESPN. The student untion hosted ESPN's Sports Center live broadcast before the UTSA vs. Tulane football game in May 2025. I am very proud of everything I have learned at the Student Union, and how successful my team has been tese past couple of years when most of us came in knowing nothing about AV.

As the Director for Rowdy CyberCon, I plan to leave my mark on the event by continuing to expand what we imagined as possible. I have been working hard to make the career fair possible like I described earlier, but I have also been attending lots of events to network and find potential sponsors, workshop presenters, or just someone who would like to see us succeed in our goals. Helping to get the Rowdy CyberCon name out there has been hugely beneficial for us already, and is opening many doors for things that we never expected to happen. We are also pushing for this to be a big community event for the entire city, not just UTSA. We started on this mission for our previous event in April, but we plan to push this even further which will not only help our event grow, but will help to strengthen the connections of the community.

Another thing myself and my co-director, Carlington, have been really pushing for is expanding our student involvement. Rowdy CyberCon is already entirely student led, organized, and executed, but mainly by cybersecurity students. We are looking to expand this and get Marketing majors to help us with PR and advertising to students. We are looking for film students to help us create really cool trailers, and recap videos that help to make a lasting impact for the event. The entire point of this event is to bring people together to make something really fun and engaging that will benefit the larger San Antonio community. Overall, I am very excited for what we have planned for the event and even more excited to see how we pull it off!

I mentioned before that I have been attending a lot of events and how helpful they have been, but I also feel a lot of imposter syndrom. Sometimes I hear people talking about a subject, and it seems like I know non of the basic terminology they are using and I wonder if I am in the right place or am going to make it in the field. Sometimes I feel like everyone around me knows way more than I could ever know, and that is probably true. Combined, they have way more experience than I could ever hope to achieve, but I have shifted towards thinking about absorbing as much knowledge as I can.

I still need to continue studying the basic fundamentals in my classes so that I can try to keep up in these conversations, but I also need to start thinking about a specialization. I am learning that "Cybersecurity" is as broad of a term as "Doctor". There are so many specializations, and no body can be expected to be an expert in all of them because it is an ever changing field, there is simply just too much happening to know everything. However, I am having troubles picturing where I seem myself in the future so that I can make a plan to get there. That is another thing these conferences have been helping me with, listening to other peoples journeys to see what I could picture myself doing.

This blog post is already getting very long, so I would like to end it with a few goals.
